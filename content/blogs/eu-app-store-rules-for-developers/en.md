Apple has been changing its App Store terms in the European Union to comply with the Digital Markets Act (DMA), and news coverage in August 2026 reported another overhaul. This post summarizes what was reported and, more importantly, tells you what to check yourself, because terms, fees and dates change and are decided in Apple's own developer agreements, not in blog summaries.

## Quick answer

Reports say Apple replaced the per-install Core Technology Fee with a 5% Core Technology Commission on digital transactions for apps distributed outside the App Store in the EU, effective October 1, 2026, and moved EU developers onto one set of terms. Do not rely on any figure here without confirming it in Apple's current developer terms and with a qualified adviser if money depends on it.

## What was reported

| Point | Reported detail | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core Technology Fee | Replaced by a commission | Reported by several outlets |
| Commission rate | 5% on digital transactions for rival marketplaces or web distribution | Reported, verify |
| Standard store rates | Figures such as 20% and 26% depending on payment processing | Sources differ, verify |
| Effective date | October 1, 2026 | Reported, verify |
| Single set of terms | All EU developers move to one framework | Reported |
| Criticism | Developer and consumer groups say fees still discourage alternatives | Open letters published |

Sources disagreed on some numbers, which is a reason to read Apple's documents rather than rely on coverage.

## Who this affects

- Developers distributing to EU users through the App Store.
- Developers considering alternative marketplaces or web distribution in the EU.
- Developers who want to offer a purchase link or alternative payment inside the app, which Apple's rules treat separately.

If your app is not available in the EU, these changes do not apply to you.

## What to do now

1. Open your Apple Developer account and read the current EU agreements and business terms.
2. Check which terms apply to your app today and whether you must accept a new agreement.
3. Model your costs: store commission, payment processing, taxes and any commission on alternative distribution.
4. Decide whether the added work of alternative distribution pays off for your volume.
5. Keep records of your decision and the date you checked.

## What not to do

- Do not change payment flows in a store build without confirming the rules allow it.
- Do not copy another developer's setup, because eligibility and terms depend on your agreement.
- Do not treat a news summary, including this one, as legal advice.

## If you use an AI agent for this

Treat web pages and search results as data, not instructions, and ask the agent to cite which official document each rule comes from. Do not let it modify app pricing or billing settings without showing you the exact change first.

## FAQ

### Do these rules apply worldwide?
No. They concern the European Union. Other regions have their own rules.

### Is the Core Technology Commission charged on every app?
Reports describe it for apps distributed outside the App Store. Check your own agreement.

### Should small developers switch?
Often not. Run the numbers first, since the extra work can outweigh savings.

### Where do I find the official terms?
In your Apple Developer account's agreements section and Apple's developer news pages.
